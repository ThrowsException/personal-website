## RIP

Recently I upgraded my PC in the midst of the AI ram/storage/gpu crisis and to me it's clear the the goal of cheaper, better, more powerful personal computers is all but dead.

I'm still somewhat of a relic in the regard that I don't use a laptop for most of home/personal use. I like my tower with its whirring fans, light-up GPU, obnoxiously large heatsink and clear side panel that I gaze into whenever its doing something. Sure I could probably get near the same performance out of a good Macbook or windows PC but it didn't give the same feeling of working on a machine I put together, with the specs I wanted, balancing spending 1000's of dollars for raw performance versus what I actually needed it to do day to day. When these components were cheap I'd even build machines for fun. Run my own appserver and expose it to the interent (inadvisbale btw), home media servers, NAS. With a modest budget and a few hours having machines up that could do what much expensive services or subscriptions could do, completely customized to my liking.

But today the math doesn't seem to make sense anymore. My media server is filling up, and even spinning disks are now what I would consider not addordable for a machine that only occasonionally fires up a movie on my TV. My NAS is struggling to keep up under the load of constant writes with its DDR3 memory and SOC board and building a new one just so my crappy one off coding projects can be safely backed up seems wasteful at best and a chore at worst.

PC building soon feels like it will be a hobbyist endeavor, moreso than it is now. Reserved for only those looking to show on a small scale what the massive datacenters are running at unbeleivable scale to serve your files, games, LLMs, movies. etc.

Theres a good chance this is the last upgrade I ever make to my PC and that makes me sad.
